# Residual-Shift Claim–Evidence Map

Frozen evidence review: 2026-09-16.

## Post-rejection comparator addendum (2026-09-23)

The historical `GO` entries below describe their **own frozen comparisons**, not superiority to the strongest published label-shift estimator. A new 34-task controlled pure-label-shift audit found that changing the EM source prior from the training fraction to the mean calibrated validation prediction, as specified by Alexandari et al. 2020, materially changed held-out risk. At target prior `.50`, mean-prior EM improved task-mean Brier by `0.002726` and logloss by `0.061157` relative to the old train-prior EM, with task-bootstrap intervals excluding zero; the effect is heterogeneous and includes near-boundary EM failures. See `../post_rejection_density_20260923/mean_prior_em_audit_report_v0.1.md`. The audit uses L2 Platt calibration, so it still does not reproduce exact BCTS. **V5.1 competitiveness against bias-corrected maximum-likelihood adaptation is unverified**; the old V5.1 full `GO` must not be used as evidence of that claim.

A subsequent matched comparison used the official `abstention` BCTS+EM code on the ten exposed CLIMB development tasks, reproducing the old Platt-source metrics to `4.84e-12` maximum absolute error. V5.1 failed the frozen competitiveness gate: its Brier/logloss intervals versus BCTS+EM crossed zero at target prior `.20`; at `.50`, its mean Brier/logloss differences were adverse (`+0.007629`, `+0.023161`) and worst-task Brier harm was `+0.053894`. See `../post_rejection_density_20260923/official_bcts_em_comparator_report_v0.1.md`. **The V5.1 superiority claim is unsupported on this external development benchmark.**
